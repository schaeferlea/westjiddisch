West Yiddish in the (Long) 19th Century: Source Situation, Sociolinguistic Setting, and Grammatical Phenomena

Follow-up project: The linguistic position of West Yiddish between German and East Yiddish (2011–2016)

Since the end of the 18th century, West Yiddish was increasingly abandoned in favor of German varieties. To date, little is known about its linguistic structures. Since there is practically no data on spoken West Yiddish collected according to linguistic criteria, written sources must be analyzed instead. Texts written in the Hebrew or Latin alphabet that are based on spoken West Yiddish exist from the 18th century onward; however, a concise account of these sources, as well as a linguistic analysis, has so far been lacking. An analysis of the linguistic structures of West Yiddish based on textual documents is a research desideratum, because it can provide new empirical evidence for the intensively debated question of the origin of Yiddish, and thus an answer to the question of the extent to which West Yiddish is (in)dependent from German. The project aims to address this by comparing West Yiddish grammatical structures with German (in its dialectal diversity) on the one hand, and with East Yiddish on the other. The project will result in two central "end products":

A description of the source situation and the sociolinguistic setting of West Yiddish in the (long) 19th century, based on an analysis of sources close to spoken language. A treatment of the question of the linguistic status of West Yiddish in relation to German and East Yiddish, including the question of the origin of Yiddish.
